# CS336 Lecture 3 Notes

> Theme: from “a Transformer that runs” to a modern LLM recipe that trains stably and deploys efficiently.

If Lecture 2 was about accounting (FLOPs, memory, MFU), Lecture 3 is about architectural tradeoffs in real systems.

---

## 1) Framing: no single “correct” architecture

The key is tradeoff management:

- stability vs speed
- quality vs cost
- long-context capability vs inference latency

---

## 2) Norm design: Pre-norm vs Post-norm

Let the sublayer function be $F(x)$.

Post-norm:

$$
y = \mathrm{LayerNorm}(x + F(x))
$$

Pre-norm:

$$
y = x + F(\mathrm{LayerNorm}(x))
$$

Why pre-norm is common in modern LLMs: smoother gradient flow and better depth scalability in practice.

---

## 3) LayerNorm vs RMSNorm

RMSNorm form:

$$
\mathrm{rms}(x) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d} x_i^2 + \varepsilon}
$$

$$
y_i = \gamma_i \cdot \frac{x_i}{\mathrm{rms}(x)}
$$

RMSNorm is often preferred for implementation simplicity and strong empirical performance in modern LLM stacks.

---

## 4) FFN and gating: GLU variants

Vanilla FFN (simplified):

$$
h = \mathrm{act}(xW_1), \quad y = hW_2
$$

GLU-style FFN (simplified):

$$
a = xW_a, \quad g = \mathrm{act}(xW_g), \quad h = a \odot g, \quad y = hW_o
$$

The gate $g$ acts as per-channel control, often improving expressiveness.

---

## 5) Layer organization: Serial vs Parallel

- **Serial**: attention then FFN.
- **Parallel**: attention and FFN branches in parallel, then merged.

Choice depends on optimization target (maturity vs throughput tradeoff exploration).

---

## 6) RoPE: rotational positional encoding

Absolute positional encoding often looks like:

$$
x = e_{\text{token}} + e_{\text{pos}}
$$

RoPE instead rotates $Q/K$ with position-dependent transforms:

$$
Q_p = R(\theta_p)Q, \quad K_t = R(\theta_t)K
$$

$$
\mathrm{score}(p,t) = Q_p^\top K_t
$$

2D rotation form:

$$
x' = x\cos\theta - y\sin\theta, \quad y' = x\sin\theta + y\cos\theta
$$

Hence pairwise dimension grouping in each head.

---

## 7) Hyperparameter couplings

Two useful structural couplings:

$$
\mathrm{model\_dim} = \mathrm{num\_heads} \times \mathrm{head\_dim}
$$

$$
\mathrm{ffn\_dim} = \mathrm{multiplier} \times \mathrm{model\_dim}
$$

Vocabulary-size tradeoff:

- large $V$: larger $V\cdot d$ parameter/output cost
- small $V$: more fragmented tokens and longer sequences

A simplified update intuition with weight decay:

$$
w \leftarrow w - \eta\nabla_w\mathcal{L} - \eta\lambda w
$$

---

## 8) Stability tricks

Common issue: overlarge logits before softmax, causing sharp distributions and unstable gradients.

Common interventions:

1. output-side stabilization (e.g., z-loss ideas)
2. attention-side normalization (e.g., QK norm)
3. logit soft-capping

---

## 9) Attention efficiency: MHA / GQA / MQA + local attention

For long context, standard attention has:

- compute pressure scaling with $L^2$
- growing KV-cache memory/bandwidth pressure

Define:

$$
H = \mathrm{num\_q\_heads}, \quad H_{kv} = \mathrm{num\_kv\_heads}
$$

- MHA: $H_{kv}=H$
- GQA: $1 < H_{kv} < H$
- MQA: $H_{kv}=1$

Interleaved attention is often used in practice:

$$
\text{full},\ \text{local},\ \text{local},\ \text{full},\dots
$$

---

## 10) Practical baseline recipe

A practical modern baseline often starts with:

- pre-norm
- RMSNorm
- GLU-style FFN
- RoPE
- GQA
- selective stabilization tricks when needed

Then iterate with small, hypothesis-driven architecture changes.
